# Round-trip a synthetic test stimulus before trusting its negative result

**Symptom:** Testing a wake-word detector by driving a speaker with a TTS-rendered phrase
(`"Hi ESP"`) produced `wake_count: 0` across 16 trials. Read at face value, that looks like a
detector defect.

**What actually happened:** The TTS engine (`tts-1`/`alloy`) rendered the short acronym `"ESP"`
inaudibly — a direct TTS-output-through-ASR round-trip check showed 13 of 16 trials came back as
just `"Hi."`, no "ESP" at all. Only a phrase repeated three times in one synthesis call reliably
rendered the acronym. The detector was never exercised with its target phrase in most trials; the
16-trial "negative result" was actually a near-total sample-size-zero result wearing a large N.

The operator's own ears caught it first — "it said 'hi hi', definitely not a coherent 'hi ESP'" —
and the round-trip check confirmed exactly that, precisely.

**The rule:** When a test's *stimulus* is itself generated by a model (TTS, a synthetic image, a
templated prompt) and the test's *content* claim rests on that stimulus meaning something specific,
verify the stimulus says/shows what you think before trusting a negative result from it. The cheap
check is usually one more model call: round-trip the generated artifact through a second model
(TTS output → ASR; a generated image → a vision-model description; a rendered PDF → OCR) and
compare against the intended content. A negative result from an unverified synthetic stimulus is
not evidence about the thing under test — it's evidence about the stimulus generator, and those are
different questions with different owners.

**Why it generalizes:** Any pipeline stage that both *generates the input* and *is upstream of the
system under test* can silently fail in a way that looks like the downstream system failing. The
generalization isn't specific to TTS/ASR — it's "don't trust N trials of a generated stimulus
without independently confirming what the stimulus actually contained," which applies to synthetic
test fixtures generally (LLM-generated test data, templated log lines, procedurally generated
audio/images used as inputs to a detector or classifier under test).
